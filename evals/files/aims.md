# Specific Aims

Cocaine use disorder affects millions of people, and no medication is approved for it. Reversal learning deficits predict relapse in human cocaine users and emerge after cocaine self-administration in rats. The striatal mechanisms that link cocaine exposure to impaired reversal remain unresolved. Our preliminary data show that phosphorylation of DARPP-32 at Thr34 in dorsomedial striatum is elevated 21 days after cocaine self-administration (n = 12 rats per group).

**Aim 1: Determine whether Thr34 phosphorylation in dorsomedial striatum is required for cocaine-induced reversal deficits.** We will use viral expression of a phospho-null mutant during abstinence and test reversal learning in an operant task.

**Aim 2: Determine whether restoring dopamine D1 receptor signaling rescues reversal performance.** We will test a D1 partial agonist across three doses in male and female rats.

**Aim 3: Identify the cell types that carry the phosphorylation change.** We will use cell-type-specific translating ribosome affinity purification in D1 and D2 Cre lines and quantify phospho-proteins.

Successful completion will identify a striatal signaling node that could be targeted to improve flexible decision making in cocaine use disorder. The work will provide the rationale and tools needed for an R01 application that tests these mechanisms across longer abstinence periods and in models of relapse.
