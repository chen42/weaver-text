# Writing-source notes

These sources document the writing principles used in Modules 03 and 04. The instructions adapt their ideas; they are not quotations or fixed algorithms. Source records and accessible material were checked on 2026-10-06. Book metadata or contents do not establish that every sentence in the skill appears in the book.

## Gopen and Swan

George D. Gopen and Judith A. Swan. "The Science of Scientific Writing." *American Scientist* 78(6), 1990, pages 550-558.

- [Bibliographic record at Duke](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/881487).
- [Full-text academic mirror at Tufts](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/george-gopen/sci.html).
- Material inspected: the article text, including its concluding structural principles and the warning against treating them as rules.
- Use in the skill: subject-verb proximity; familiar information for context and linkage; emphasis in stress positions; actions expressed through verbs; and alignment of grammatical emphasis with intended meaning.
- Limit: these are reader-expectation principles. The article explicitly rejects a fixed algorithm for good writing. It does not support the skill imposing numerical sentence-length or word-spacing rules.

## Williams and Bizup

Joseph M. Williams and Joseph Bizup. *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*, 13th edition. Pearson.

- [Publisher record and contents](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/Williams-Style-Lessons-in-Clarity-and-Grace-RENTAL-EDITION-13th-Edition/P200000002140?view=educator).
- Material inspected: the publisher's description and contents, including Actions, Characters, Cohesion and Coherence, Emphasis, and Concision.
- Use in the skill: reader-oriented sentence construction, clear subjects and verbs, connections between sentences, and concision.
- Limit: the full book was not inspected. The skill's practical wording is an adaptation, not a verified quotation from this edition. The related subject/action principles were also checked in Gopen and Swan's article.

## Schimel

Joshua Schimel. *Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded*. Oxford University Press.

- [Publisher record](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/writing-science-9780199760244).
- [Author's explanation of opening, challenge, action, and resolution](https://schimelwritingscience.wordpress.com/2021/10/06/writing-science-getting-started-group-exercise/).
- Material inspected: the author's exercise explaining how context, a question, results, and a conclusion connect, with attention to readers outside the immediate specialty.
- Use in the skill: organizing a scientific argument around the problem, question, work, and supported conclusion or anticipated contribution.
- Limit: the full book was not inspected and the publisher page could not be extracted directly. The exercise's paragraph and sentence counts are training instructions, not universal limits. Proposed grant work is not described as completed evidence.

## Alley

Michael Alley. *The Craft of Scientific Writing*, 4th edition. Springer, 2018. DOI: 10.1007/978-1-4419-8288-9.

- [Publisher record and overview](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4419-8288-9).
- Material inspected: publisher metadata and the overview discussing precise sentences, emphasis, organization, and connections between ideas.
- Use in the skill: clear scientific exposition and readable connections without a compulsory sentence-length pattern.
- Limit: the full book was not inspected. Assertion-evidence presentation methods are not treated as a requirement for every paper heading or grant aim.
